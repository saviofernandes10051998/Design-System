Case study · DePaul University

Helping volunteers find the right reflection, faster.

The VIA (Vincentians in Action) Reflection Toolkit is DePaul’s free library of reflections, icebreakers and facilitation tips for people doing service. I manage the site. This is how I studied its problems and made the case for its future.

My role
Website manager & UX designer
Timeline
Spring–Summer 2025
Tools
WordPress, Google Analytics 4, Figma
Audience
Students, staff and service leaders
Long story short

The toolkit had great content that was hard to find, aging, and rarely promoted. I audited the site, analyzed its visitor data, and turned the findings into a plain-language case for keeping it, plus a design concept that lets people filter reflections by pillar, time and group size.

01 · The problem

Good content, hard to reach

The toolkit lives on DePaul’s WordPress blog platform. Over time its content aged, it was barely promoted, and the platform limited what we could change. Its future was being questioned.

A facilitator with ten minutes before a group meeting can’t scroll through a long blog to find the right reflection.

The key question became: how can people quickly find a reflection that fits their group, and how do we show the toolkit is worth keeping?

02 · Process

Four steps

  1. Audit

    Reviewed the live site for usability and accessibility problems.

  2. Analyze

    Built a Google Analytics 4 report and a data comparison of how people use the site.

  3. Present

    Made a case-for-continuation brochure in plain, non-technical language for stakeholders.

  4. Propose

    Designed a “reflection finder” concept to make content faster to find.

03 · Audit findings

What got in the way

From my review of the live home page.

High

One long page, little separation

Navigation, content and footer run together, so the main content is hard to scan.

High

Search is easy to miss

The search field has no visible button and only a small “Search for:” label.

Medium

Missing alt text

The hero image and many pillar icons have no alt text, so screen reader users miss them.

Medium

Inconsistent pillar links

Six pillars link to tag pages, but one links to a category page, so results look different.

Medium

Placeholder links

Several footer links go nowhere (“#”), which hurts trust.

05 · Proposed concept

A reflection finder

Instead of one long blog page, each resource becomes a card that says what it is, which pillars it supports, how long it takes, and what group size it fits. Pillar tags work as filters.

07 · Lessons learned

What I’d do differently

  • Talk to users earlier. Analytics showed what people did, not why. Short interviews with facilitators would fill that gap.
  • Speak the audience’s language. Plain sentences convinced stakeholders more than charts did.
  • Design within the platform. Knowing WordPress’s limits early kept the concept realistic.
